# Guide for Brainstorming Session: Solution Optimization for GitHub Copilot

Please use this guide as part of your developer productivity brainstorming session with the customer. 

## Core Principles

- Unlike with some other engagements or activities there isn’t necessarily a right and a wrong outcome to this session 
- Your main role in the session is to facilitate a conversation between different customer stakeholders about developer productivity. Guide the conversation and facilitate but don’t override the customer stakeholders’ points or railroad the conversation into a particular direction.
- You are in the session to help the customer discover how they are measuring developer productivity today and how they may want to measure it in future. You can provide advice based on your experience and evidence from Microsoft’s product teams. 
- Be clear about where your advice and guidance are coming from. Quote your sources. 
- Ensure that the customer knows that just because something works at Microsoft it doesn’t mean that the same approach will work for their organization or their team. 

## Overall structure of the session

-  We recommend that you split the session into dedicated and time boxed sections. We recommend roughly an hour for each part. 
- The activity your customer is going into requires deep thinking and it’s recommended to take a break between each part of the session.
- The brainstorming session should happen after the knowledge transfer on developer productivity has occurred. You should reference the material presented in the session as a conversation starter. 
- The participants should be folks that have the necessary decision power to change the way their organization measures developer productivity. If the customer is open to it they can also include some developers to incorporate their perspective. 
   - Be sure to discuss this option during the scoping call. It is important that you create an environment that is psychologically safe for everyone. The CSAM can give you advice on the customer’s culture and how open they will likely (not) be to include various different roles in the discussion. If unsure let the customer drive the audience selection for the session after explaining the concept in some detail.


## Sample agenda for brainstorming session

- 15 minutes: Introductions and warm up
- 1 hour: Assessing the status quo
- 15 minutes: break
- 1 hour: Designing the desired state
- 15 minutes: Close out

Note that if all stakeholders have not participated in the knowledge transfer session ahead of the brainstorming session, then you may need to incorporate a quick knowledge transfer recap into your agenda. We strongly recommend to design this around the customer’s unique situation, industry, and interests. Discuss this during the scoping call to maximize clarity around this topic.

## Introductions and Warm Up

- Make sure everyone knows each other and perform a short ice breaker.
   - Examples: 
       - What are you hoping to get out of the session today?
       - What’s your definition of developer productivity?
- If delivering this session in person and folks aren’t familiar with one another you may ask them to create name tags. 

## Assessing the Status Quo

- Bearing in mind the different developer productivity standards discussed in relation to GitHub Copilot throughout the session, ask the group what they are measuring today
- You may want to collect their responses in writing first so that you can group and categorize them.
   - If delivering in person you can use post its and a whiteboard to achieve this.
   - If delivering remotely you can use the chat, the whiteboard, or the polling feature in Teams along with some third party tools as long as they are approved for use. 
- Once the initial results are collected look for patterns. 
- Discuss with the group if everyone in the organization gave the same or different answers. 
- Ask them to discuss if having a unified standard would be better or worse for the organization. 
- If required, ask the group to rate (can be done anonymously) how well they are currently tracking their developer productivity. 
- If necessary, go back to the knowledge sharing session content for conversation starters around the topic. You can ask leading questions such as “When deploying a new developer tool, how do you measure if people like to use it or not?” or “How does the metric you mentioned help measure if a developer is effective? Does everyone agree with the statement?”

## Timeboxing and Breaks

- Brainstorming is taxing and you want to make sure your participants get a reasonable break between the different sections of the session. 
- Timeboxing is important. Stop discussions that are going around in circles or that are likely to end up focusing on a particular topic very deeply while ignoring the bigger picture. 
- While you don’t need to break up a fruitful discussion we recommend that you keep the conversation pacey, relevant, and on-time so that each participant ( no matter where they are in the organization ) has a good experience. 

## Designing the desired state

- We recommend that you once again collect opinions in an anonymous or semi-anonymous way and group them. 
- If folks need a little bit of help to come up with ideas you can project one of the slides used during knowledge sharing. Be sure to point out that what works for Microsoft might not necessarily work for their organization and vice versa. 
- Following the collection of ideas, group them to create time boxed discussion topics. 

- Depending on the responses you might either see lots of agreement or disagreement. 
- If there is lots of agreement you may not need a lot of discussion at this stage and can instead start down the process of helping the customer team document the new metrics they’d like to introduce and why. You don’t necessarily need to finish this together but writing words on a page can be powerful. 
    - You can send them the (half-)finished document following the engagement so that they can continue working offline. 
     - If working on a whiteboard, take a picture of it and include it in your close out. 

- In cases where there are opposing views, it’s important to spend the time to discuss them through. Instead of finger pointing ask an open question like “Would anyone like to add some context here?” or “These two don’t seem to go together. Let’s discuss. Would anyone like to start?”
  - Instead of providing your opinion in the discussion, take a step back and facilitate. You can give your recommendations when asked but always be clear to point out that the stakeholders in the room know their organization best. 

As with the previous section of the brainstorming session this should be a time-boxed discussion. Prioritize discussion over drafting the customer’s developer productivity metric. If you have time to do it in the session then this is a plus.

## Close out

- Be sure to thank everyone for their time
- Collect screenshots/photos of whiteboards, submissions, etc. to share with the customer as part of the engagement close out
- Save any of the work you created during the session and share it with the customer
